Melancholy Boughs

By: Zainab Ammar

I beheld a tree, her trunk was laced in despair,
Splitting asunder, and I pondered if it was the doing of a cruel air.
How grievous, how agonizing
I repeated on her behalf and stood there, analyzing.
Yet she stood tall, embraced by neighbors, by other trees,
Lifted so high, as if it were her pain they’re trying to ease.
I wondered if she weighed heavy, a burden for them,
A burden upon their branches.
If given the choice, would they condemn?
Is it a weight to wield your power in stitching together another's soul,
Uncertain if it could ever be whole?
Do they possess the fortitude to cradle her forevermore?
For it must be a heavy toll to bear all this timber and whatever for?
They sought to alleviate her suffering,
Hoping she’ll start recovering,
Yearning for signs that she’ll mend and revive,
Yet her burden will shatter those who hold tight; they may not quite survive.
But will they continue to bear her weight as they wither in life?
Or will they abandon her to her own strife?
