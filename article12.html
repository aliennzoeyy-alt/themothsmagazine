Do I Walk Like a Snail?

By: Zainab Ammar

If you often feel like everyone is moving forward in life as fast as a falcon, with such a determination for success, while you glide slowly on a single muscular foot akin to a snail, this article is for you.

You spend hours on your social media platforms scrolling and seeing how everyone is doing much better than you while you're stuck in the exact same place. I won't tell you that social media is fake and those people are not living an ideal life and they have their own problems which they don't share, for that is common knowledge now and it's very stupid to say this. They just don't spend hours scrolling, and they don't use their phones as much as you do. But don't you worry, I'm here to teach you how to reduce your scrolling time and be productive, for being so absorbed in brainrot reduces your ability to create and maintain a healthy life.

Consume in Order to Create

Work on your brain: knowledge is your weapon

Read. Don't say "I hate books," don't say "I don't have time"—you have all the time in the world. And don't say "I suffer from severe brainrot." It will take you a lot of energy to focus at first, but you can always start with small, tiny books. As a start, reading does not only increase your knowledge of the world around you; it makes you more empathetic and provides you the chance to live many lives and walk in different people's shoes to be able to experience and live in different realities. It is critical for human beings to read, for it gives you the wisdom and the courage of lived experience. You do not only learn from your mistakes and the mistakes of those around you, you will also learn from different characters in a completely different world. And at last on your deathbed, you will be able to rest knowing that you did not only live one life.

There is a treasure called YouTube

Take online courses. YouTube is full of free online courses for different majors in STEM, political science, philosophy, etc. There are even online courses for Excel (I highly recommend MIT courses); get back on track and work on your skills. I don't care if you chose that major randomly—figure it out, find something interesting in it, and dive deep into it. There is no major on this planet that does not have something very interesting in it. Also, it does not make any sense for you to waste four years or more and graduate from a major that you don't know anything about, even if you were forced into it. However, you can take any courses you want online, even if they are unrelated to your major, and I mean ANYTHING. It's all there. YouTube contains a huge amount of courses that you will need for developing a new skill.

Volunteer

If there is any event that requires a number of volunteers in your city, VOLUNTEER! This would look awesome on your resume, not to mention the remarkable feeling of providing a great service for locals. You will meet a lot of people and gain a lot of experience, so I beseech you, VOLUNTEER.

I would also highly recommend replacing scrolling on Instagram/TikTok with scrolling through Pinterest and Substack, for those apps are full of inspiration and ideas which will motivate you into doing your tasks professionally and perfectly. In addition, I recommend downloading Letterboxd and watching movies and shows while keeping track of them, since they play a crucial role in developing your taste, your emotional intelligence, critical thinking, and the ability to analyze what you watch.

Now enough with the consumption of the brain. Time for it to create!

The Art of Creation Will Save You

Do you have any hobbies? Let me guess: you used to paint, then you completely neglected your art. Don't worry, I'm not judging, I'm here to guide you. First, if you don't have any hobbies, you must find one. Try crochet? (I myself am learning crochet). Who knows, perhaps you would do an awesome job and open your own crochet shop? Or try clay? Make some whimsical pieces with it. Or maybe write? Whether it's poetry, short stories, or even journaling. Or maybe try to bake something? All of the things I've mentioned, and which I failed to mention, are considered hobbies and they will make you feel much, much better than doomscrolling. That whole satisfaction of creating a piece of art will save you; it will guide you and teach you how to live properly. I'm not saying don't ever check your phone or messages. I'm just saying that mindlessly scrolling on your phone is a waste of time, and time is precious. Your time is precious—every second of it, do not waste it.
