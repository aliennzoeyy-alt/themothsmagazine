Growing Wings to Go Your Own Way, or Is It for Your Final Decay?

By: Zainab Ammar

Although we humans are born on the Earth's surface from a womb of flesh and bone, we still do not differ much from a dragonfly born underwater from an egg. We remain on the surface; we spend the majority of our lives searching for our own glorious purpose. Some of us even remain stuck in one place, waiting for something to give us meaning in our lives, bewildered by our own mortality, searching for something that will make us levitate. The same goes for dragonflies: they spend the majority of their lives underwater, flightless, waiting for the day they grow wings and fly.

Dragonflies shed their skin to grow larger, disposing of old shapes just like we grow larger in age, and as the years pass by we dispose of old clothes and even of old memories sometimes. Until one day it becomes unbearable for dragonflies to keep on living underwater, and in the dead of night or early morning, they crawl out of the water, finding refuge on a plant stem or a rock. During this process, their hard outer skin splits open, and the dragonfly pulls itself out of the shell, paving its path for growth, transformation, and freedom. Us humans, we do not crawl out of a shell to fly. Instead, we experience this transformation internally, mentally, and spiritually. In the dead of night, we make life changing decisions: we decide to grow, change, and leave our environment to fly to a brand new one and start a brand new life.

Sadly dragonflies can only experience flying in the air for a very short period of time. They only live one to two months in the air, for that environment is too cruel, too ruthless and too merciless for such a tender creature. Their soft wings get fractured by rain easily; they are cold blooded and need sunlight to fly, so cold temperatures prevent them from hunting, and they die of starvation. Their poetically melancholic life cycle made me ponder about how changes are necessary and how agonizingly difficult it is to foresee the right decision. As humans we change, we grow, and we have always sought freedom and transformation to the level it became essential for us to continue living. But what if we make the wrong decision? What if that growth leads to our demise? Just like the dragonflies, what if the price for freedom is death?

Although our bodies are not as sensitive as the dragonflies', there are still some things that consume our souls and hearts. There are still things that kill us within in that kingdom of the surface world, and the everlasting question remains: what if finally finding your glorious purpose is the reason you get cast out of that theatre of life?
